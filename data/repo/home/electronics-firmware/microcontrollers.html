Microcontrollers

ARM Cortex‑M, AVR, RISC‑V, ESP32 and RP2040

ARM Cortex‑M

  • Cortex‑M0 / M0+ Ultra‑low‑power 32‑bit cores for simple, cost‑sensitive applications. Used in many entry‑level MCUs from NXP, ST, and Silicon Labs.
  • Cortex‑M3 / M4 Balanced performance and power efficiency. M4 includes a FPU and DSP extensions, making it ideal for motor control and audio applications.
  • Cortex‑M7 / M33 High‑performance cores with caches and branch prediction. M33 adds TrustZone for secure applications and is used in industrial and IoT edge devices.

AVR

  • Classic AVR 8‑bit RISC architecture with a large family (ATmega, ATtiny). Famous for Arduino Uno (ATmega328P), simple peripherals, and extensive community support.
  • AVR‑DA / AVR‑DB Modern AVR devices with improved analog features (op‑amps, DACs), event system, and higher clock speeds – bridging 8‑bit simplicity with modern peripherals.

RISC‑V

  • Open Standard ISA A free and open RISC instruction set architecture. Growing ecosystem with cores from SiFive, Western Digital, and many open‑source implementations.
  • Embedded RISC‑V RISC‑V MCUs are emerging in the market (e.g. GD32V, CH32V). They offer a modern alternative to ARM with comparable performance and toolchain support.

ESP32

  • ESP32‑S / C Series Dual‑core Xtensa or RISC‑V processors with integrated Wi‑Fi and Bluetooth. The S2, S3, and C3 series target different performance and power profiles.
  • ESP‑IDF Framework Espressif's official development framework with FreeRTOS, extensive drivers, and a rich set of examples for IoT and connected applications.

RP2040

  • Dual‑Core Cortex‑M0+ Raspberry Pi's custom MCU with two cores, 264 kB SRAM, and a flexible PIO state machine for bit‑banging high‑speed protocols.
  • Raspberry Pi Pico The reference board for RP2040, popular in education and hobbyist projects. Supports MicroPython, C/C++, and Arduino with excellent community support.
