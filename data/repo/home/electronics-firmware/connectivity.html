Wireless communication protocols and interfaces

Bluetooth Low Energy / Bluetooth

Bluetooth is a short-range wireless communication standard for exchanging data over distances up to 100 meters. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) is a power-efficient variant introduced in Bluetooth 4.0, designed for IoT devices, wearables, and sensors. BLE uses a 2.4 GHz ISM band with 40 channels, supports connection-oriented and broadcast modes, and features low-duty-cycle operation for extended battery life.

  • BLE 5.0+: Increased range (4×), speed (2×), and broadcast capacity (8×).
  • GATT: Generic Attribute Profile for service and characteristic discovery.
  • Advertising: Broadcast beacons (iBeacon, Eddystone) for proximity and location.
  • HID over GATT: Used for keyboards, mice, and other human interface devices.
  • Mesh: Bluetooth Mesh for large-scale device networks (lighting, etc.).

USB‑RF

USB-RF refers to wireless communication using USB dongles with proprietary or standard RF protocols in the sub-1 GHz or 2.4 GHz bands. Common implementations include 2.4 GHz modules (nRF24L01, CC2500) and sub-1 GHz modules (CC1101, Si4463). USB-RF is often used for wireless keyboards, mice, game controllers, and remote controls where low latency and low cost are prioritized over standardization.

  • nRF24L01+: 2.4 GHz transceiver with 250 kbps, 1 Mbps, 2 Mbps data rates.
  • CC1101: Sub-1 GHz (315/433/868/915 MHz) with excellent range and penetration.
  • Protocols: Proprietary, Enhanced ShockBurst, or Nordic's Gazell.
  • USB HID: USB-RF dongles often present as standard HID devices to the host.
  • Low latency: Typical latency < 5 ms for gaming peripherals.
