Lubricants

Chemical and physical properties of PFPE greases, oils, and dry film lubricants

Terminology Note
Viscosity is the measure of a fluid's internal resistance to flow, defined as the ratio of shear stress to shear rate. In Newtonian fluids (like pure oils), viscosity remains constant regardless of applied shear force. In non-Newtonian systems (like greases), viscosity decreases under shear (shear-thinning), allowing the material to flow when stressed but remain in place otherwise.

Density is the mass per unit volume (ρ = m/V). Perfluoropolyether (PFPE) molecules contain heavy fluorine atoms, resulting in a remarkably high density of approximately 1.8–1.9 g/cm³—almost twice that of water. In contrast, polysiloxane (silicone) oils have a density of ~0.97 g/cm³.

A grease is a colloidal dispersion—a semi-solid system where a thickener (typically PTFE microparticles) forms a three-dimensional sponge-like matrix that immobilizes the base oil via surface tension and capillary forces. Under mechanical shear, the matrix deforms and releases the oil. A pure oil lacks this structural matrix and flows freely as a continuous liquid phase.

NLGI consistency grade classifies grease stiffness from 000 (fluid) to 6 (solid block) based on penetration tests. Grade 0 is semi-fluid, achieved by a lower thickener concentration compared to grade 2. In keyboards, grade 0 greases are preferred because they spread easily into thin films without manually working the grease.

Krytox product codes follow the pattern GPL 2XX gY: the first digit after "2" indicates the base oil viscosity (0 ≈ 60 cSt, 5 ≈ 550 cSt at 20 °C), and gY indicates the NLGI grade (e.g. g0 = NLGI 0). All PFPE lubricants listed here are chemically inert, non-conductive, and non-reactive with plastics due to the strength of the carbon-fluorine bond.

Perfluoropolyether (PFPE) Greases

  • Krytox GPL 205g0 An NLGI grade 0 grease formulated from a high-viscosity PFPE base oil (ISO VG 220, ~550 cSt at 20 °C) and a PTFE thickener. The 'g0' denotes its semi-fluid consistency. The high base oil viscosity provides substantial hydrodynamic film thickness, resulting in high damping and a low coefficient of friction under load. Well-suited for linear and tactile switch components, but its high viscosity makes it unsuitable for coil springs, where a low-viscosity oil is preferred to prevent sluggish compression.
  • Tribosys 3204 A semi-fluid (NLGI 0) colloidal dispersion of a medium-viscosity PFPE base oil and PTFE thickener. The moderate base oil viscosity yields a thinner hydrodynamic film compared to GPL 205. This provides effective friction reduction and acoustic damping without excessive energy dissipation, making it ideal for tactile mechanisms where the restoring force must overcome film resistance without dampening the tactile event.
  • Tribosys 3203 A lighter semi-fluid PFPE-PTFE grease formulated with a lower-viscosity base oil. It generates a minimal hydrodynamic film, offering low shear strength. This reduces boundary friction without significantly altering the dynamic response or acoustic profile of the system. Used where maximum lubrication with minimal viscous drag is required, such as on linear sliding contacts.

Perfluoropolyether (PFPE) Oils

  • Krytox GPL 105 A pure, unthickened PFPE oil with a kinematic viscosity of ~60 cSt at 20 °C. Exhibits Newtonian fluid behavior. Suitable for low-load, high-frequency components (such as coil springs) where surface tension is sufficient to retain a thin film. Inadequate for high-shear sliding contacts, as the liquid readily migrates away from the friction zone due to the lack of a structural thickener matrix.
  • Krytox GPL 103 A highly fluid PFPE oil with a kinematic viscosity of ~20 cSt at 20 °C. Its low molecular weight results in low intermolecular forces, rapid spreading, and high capillary action. Often utilized for mass-coating springs via bag-lubrication or for lightweight sliding contacts where minimizing viscous drag is prioritized over long-term film durability and adhesion.

Polysiloxane (Silicone) Greases

  • Silicone Grease / Dielectric Grease A polysiloxane-based grease typically thickened with amorphous silica or PTFE. Polysiloxanes feature a siloxane (Si–O) backbone, which provides high thermal stability and excellent dielectric strength. The low surface tension of silicone allows it to wet surfaces effectively. It is highly water-repellent and electrically insulating, making it ideal for stabilizer wire channels, though it can cause stress cracking or swelling in certain polycarbonates and plastics if not formulated carefully.

Solid / Dry Film Lubricants

  • PTFE (Polytetrafluoroethylene) A solid synthetic fluoropolymer. As a dry film, PTFE consists of linear macromolecules with a rigid, helical structure due to fluorine atom repulsion. This configuration results in a remarkably low coefficient of friction (≈0.05–0.10) because the smooth molecular profiles slide over one another with minimal intermolecular adhesion (interfacial slip). It functions as a boundary lubricant that adheres lightly to substrates, though it wears under repeated sliding contact. As a thickener in greases, PTFE microparticles form the structural network that immobilizes the base oils.
